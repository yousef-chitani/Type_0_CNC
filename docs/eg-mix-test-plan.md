## 3. Starting recipes

Blends are by mass of the dry aggregate. The whiting stays at 10 % or less (its fine part raises the epoxy demand).

| Blend | Quartz 2–5 | Quartz 1–3 | Sand 0–1 | Whiting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 (baseline) | 50 % | – | 40 % | 10 % |
| P2 (coarser) | 55 % | – | 35 % | 10 % |
| P3 (finer) | 45 % | – | 45 % | 10 % |
| P4 (gap filled) | 35 % | 20 % | 35 % | 10 % |

P4 needs a bag of GCL 1–3 mm. Only buy it if you want to test it; otherwise run P1–P3.

What each epoxy level means. The packed stone must leave no more empty space than the epoxy can fill:

| Epoxy by mass | Epoxy by volume | Dry tamped density the blend must reach | Density of a void-free brick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 % | 20.4 % | 2.11 kg/L | 2.34 kg/L |
| 11 % | 22.2 % | 2.06 kg/L | 2.32 kg/L |
| 12 % | 23.9 % | 2.02 kg/L | 2.29 kg/L |
| 13 % | 25.6 % | 1.97 kg/L | 2.27 kg/L |

For 1 kg of mix at 11 %: 110 g binder (87.3 g resin + 22.7 g hardener) and 890 g aggregate (P1: 445 g quartz, 356 g sand, 89 g whiting).

## 4. Packing test (dry, no epoxy, about 1 hour)

1. Weigh an empty 1 L straight-sided tub. Fill it with water to the brim and weigh again: that gives its true volume.
2. Weigh out 2.5 kg of blend P1 and mix it dry in a bucket.
3. Fill the dry tub in 3 layers, tamping each layer 25 times with a flat-ended rod. Strike off level and weigh.
4. Dry tamped density = stone mass ÷ tub volume. Do it twice and average.
5. Repeat for P2, P3 (and P4).

**Use the densest blend.** Read off the table above which epoxy level it can reach. If no blend reaches 2.02 kg/L, expect 13 % and tell the session: the epoxy quantity is then tight (section 9).

The stone is not wasted: it goes back in the bags.

## 6. Mixing and placing (the same method as the real castings)

1. Dry-mix the aggregate for the batch in the bucket with the paddle mixer (F17), about 1 minute. Dust mask on.
2. Weigh resin and hardener (100 : 26) to within 1 %. Stir 2–3 minutes, scraping the sides and bottom.
3. Pour the binder onto the stone **straight away**. Never leave mixed resin standing in the bucket: in a mass it heats up and runs away.
4. Mix 3–4 minutes, until every stone is wet and there are no dry pockets. At 10–12 % it looks like damp sand, not a liquid.
5. First, pack a thin layer of **fine mix** (the same recipe without the 2–5 mm quartz) against the mould floor, the inserts and under any flat bar. Press it in by hand (gloved).
6. Place the mix in **layers of 30–50 mm**. Tamp each layer with a flat-ended rod or a block, edges and corners first, until the surface closes and a sheen of resin shows. Rough up the top of each layer lightly before the next.
7. Note the time each batch was mixed and when it was placed.

Batch size for the real castings: about 10–12 kg per bucket. The base is 7–8 batches and about 3 layers; with one person mixing and one tamping, about 3 hours.

## 8. How to judge each test

**A bricks (epoxy level).** Weigh each brick and measure it: density = mass ÷ volume. Saw it in half and look at the cut faces.
- Pass: density within 3 % of the void-free figure in section 3; no void larger than 2 mm inside; no more than about 5 small voids on a 100 × 100 face; no resin layer thicker than 2 mm at the top and no band of bare coarse stone at the bottom.
- **Choose the lowest epoxy level that passes.** If two pass, the lower one is stiffer and creeps less.

**C heat brick.** Wrap the four sides in 50 mm of insulation so heat only leaves through the top and bottom, like the real slab. Log the centre probe and the air for 3 days.
- Record the peak and when it happens. The estimate in `curing-heat.md` is a rise of about 20–35 °C for this wrapped brick.
- If the centre goes above about 60 °C, the real pours need cooler materials, no tent for the first day, or a fan.

**C pull test.** Bridge the brick with a steel bar resting on its two ends (100 mm or more clear of the cast-in bar). Put the M12 rod through it and tighten a nut with a torque wrench in steps: 20, 30, 40, 50 N·m. 50 N·m is about 2 tonnes of pull, above the working preload.
- Pass: no cracks, the rod end doesn't creep up (dial indicator on it), and the thread still runs freely afterwards.

**D beam section.** Saw across at one-third and two-thirds of the length.
- Pass: the 30 mm web between the cores, the 25 mm web to the channel and the 20 mm skins are filled, with no void larger than 2 mm; the cores have not moved more than 2 mm; no gap under the insert bar.
- Fail: go to one Ø40 core, or a finer mix around the cores, and cut another section.

**E rail-seat strip.**
- After the post-cure, lay a straightedge along the bar and measure the gap with feeler gauges or the dial indicator. Review builds saw 0.1–0.3 mm over 770 mm. If it is more than about 0.2 mm over 500 mm, plan to split the real bars into 2–3 pieces.
- Level the strip with the precision level. Tape a dam along the open side. Mix about 200 g of neat Ampreg, let it stand 5 minutes for bubbles to rise, and pour the seat 5 mm deep.
- After 7 days: flatness along the seat with the straightedge and dial indicator; look for a raised edge (meniscus) at the dam and for dimples.
- After the post-cure: measure again. Neat Ampreg shrinks 1.64 % in length, so look for the seat pulling away from the dam or cracking.
- Bolt a rail on at its normal torque for 2 weeks, remove it, and look for dents.
- Pass: flat within about 0.1 mm over 500 mm with no cracks or dents. That is good enough to finish by shimming or scraping; it will not reach 0.01–0.02 mm on its own.

**B prisms (stiffness).** Rest the prism flat (25 mm tall) on two steel rods 280 mm apart. Dial indicator under the centre. Hang 25 kg, read; hang 50 kg, read.
- Stiffness in GPa = 2.15 ÷ (extra deflection in mm between 25 and 50 kg).
- Commercial EG is 28–35 GPa; a poor hobby mix is about 14 GPa. **Pass: 25 GPa or more** (0.086 mm or less).
- Compare B1 (room-cured) with B2 (post-cured).
- Optional creep check: leave B2 loaded with 25 kg for a week somewhere warm (30–35 °C) and note any extra deflection. With a post-cured Tg of 74 °C this matters much less than it did with GlassCast.

## 10. Kit the tests need

Not on the parts list yet. Tell the session which you already have; the rest go into a small price check.

- Kitchen or parcel **scales**: 0–5 kg reading to 1 g (binder), and 0–30 kg (aggregate).
- **Steel straightedge**, 500 mm or longer, and feeler gauges.
- **Torque wrench** covering 20–50 N·m.
- A way to **saw EG**: angle grinder with a diamond disc (dust mask, outdoors).
- Buckets (4–5), mixing sticks, measuring tub, flat-ended tamping rod.
- **PPE:** nitrile gloves, eye protection, FFP3 dust mask for the dry stone and whiting, long sleeves. Epoxy sensitises skin: no bare-hand contact.
- 50 mm insulation offcuts for the heat brick, and the tent with its heater and thermostat (`curing-heat.md`).
- Weights for the prism test: 2 × 25 kg (the quartz or sand bags do the job).

## 11. What goes into the record

One line per piece: date, blend, epoxy %, batch masses, room and material temperature, humidity, time mixed, time placed, release used, demould notes, mass, size, density, and a photo of each cut face. Put the photos and the temperature log in the repo so the session can read them.
