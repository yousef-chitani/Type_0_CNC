# Video notes: "How to Build Epoxy Granite Machine Base" (17 Nov 2026)

Source: the owner's transcript of a YouTube video (auto-captions, so some words are wrong; percentages are by weight). One hobby builder. **Nothing here is decided.** The questions at the end are for the owner.

Compared against the current design: the cast tub (D-137, D-139, `moving-gantry-sketch.md`: 128 kg solid, about 11–12 % Ampreg, hand-tamped). `eg-moulds.md` and `eg-mix-test-plan.md` still describe the old base and portal; that is not fixed here.

## What the video does

- **The aluminium carries the stiffness; the EG only damps and gives the shape.** Machined aluminium reference surfaces are joined by an aluminium skeleton inside the mould, and the EG is poured round it.
- **Seven test pieces.** Sand + stone + 12–14 % epoxy had voids every time. Vacuum degassing made it worse (it frothed up, about 25 % lighter). Sieving out the fines gave a smoother face. Dropping the stone helped most.
- **Final mix: 80 % sand, 20 % epoxy, about 0.25 % black dye.** No stone. Pourable. Vibrated on a shaker table for the whole working time (40 minutes); bubbles started rising after about 5 minutes.
- **Mixing order:** sand, then epoxy part A (mix 2–3 min), then dye, then part B (mix 5–10 min) with a drill paddle in a tall bucket. Pour slowly into the mould.
- **Mould:** washed, wax release in 2 coats (no spots missed), wax sticks pressed into the inside corners as fillets, tape over threaded holes in the skeleton, no wax on the skeleton. Quick heat-gun passes on the top after vibrating to pop bubbles.
- **Shaker table:** rubber feet, plywood deck (12 mm, doubled if needed), shaker motor, about $100.
- **Largest stone under 1/3 of the thinnest section.** Slow epoxy: less heat, less mould warping.
- **The only result shown is the surface finish.** No stiffness, damping or cut-section check.

## Where it differs

1. **The final recipe does not suit the tub.** In the video the aluminium is the structure. In the tub the EG is the structure (pass mark 25 GPa on the prisms). Sand-only EG at 20 % epoxy is less stiff, shrinks more and gets hotter. At 20 % the 128 kg tub needs about **25.6 kg of epoxy, more than the 22.68 kg kit** (at 11–12 %: 14–15 kg).
2. **Mixing order.** The video adds part A to the sand first and part B last. That avoids mixed resin standing in a bucket (the runaway risk in `epoxy-options.md`). The risk: part B may not spread evenly through a stiff 11 % mix. Also, the docs disagree with each other: `eg-mix-test-plan.md` mixes resin and hardener first; `eg-moulds.md` step 2 says "wet the aggregate with resin first".
3. **Vibration time.** The video vibrates for the full working time. `eg-moulds.md` (D-77, D-123) says vibrate each layer briefly, because long vibration lets the coarse stone sink. The video had no coarse stone, so both can be right.
4. **Mixing time.** The video mixes 5–10 min after part B; the test plan says 3–4 min.

## Where it could help: the beam (D-143)

An aluminium box filled with EG for damping is the video's case. Rough numbers for the sketch beam (150 × 130 × 10, 890 long): inside 130 × 110, about 12.7 L.
- Filled with the tub mix (2.3 kg/L): about **29 kg**. Filled with 80/20 sand mix (about 2.0 kg/L, ASSUMED): about **25 kg**, with about 5 kg of epoxy.
- Either way the moving mass in Y goes from about 50 kg to about 75–80 kg, and the epoxy kit would not cover it on top of the tub.
- So: input for the beam task, not a choice. A partial fill or a damping strip are options that task can weigh.
