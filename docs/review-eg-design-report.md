# Review: Type 0 epoxy-granite design vs real builds (2026-11-09)

Scope: the task in `review-task-eg-design.md`. I read the design files and the CAD script (`cad/v2/assembly.py`), checked the geometry, and compared it with build logs, supplier data and industry data.

**Limits of this review**
- The owner saved the blocked pages from a browser, and I read them: the CNCArena inserts thread, V1E, Eng-Tips, Fibreglast, and the whole CNCZone/CNCArena EG mega-thread except pages 3 and 5 (125 of 127 pages, about 5,000 posts, January 2007 to November 2024). MyCNCUK is still offline, so its claims stay *unverified*.
- I read pages 1–41 post by post. For pages 42–127 (3,416 posts) I searched for each topic of this review and read the posts that matched. A post with no matching words can be missed. Thread posts are cited as [C1 page #post].
- The thread is mostly material research and test samples. The finished machines with useful detail are DaBit's gantry mill (2014), walter's failed mini-mill base (2007) and a 10 m commercial base (castinite, 2011).
- I found no source for "Bootstrap Bill" or the lostzeros mini-mill.
- `cad/v2/out/base_cores_plan.png`, `base_cores_sections.png` and `castings_tapers.png` are not in this checkout. The base-core review uses the numbers in the task file only.

## 1. Summary

1. **The portal webs are too thin to fill.** In the CAD, the two Ø50 cores are 10 mm apart, and 10 mm behind the X-screw channel floor. The skins are 20 mm. With 2–5 mm quartz, this is 2 to 4 stones wide. RAMPF gives 40 mm minimum wall for 5 mm grain. This is the highest risk in the plan.
2. **The resin is soft for a machine frame.** GlassCast 50 PLUS has Tg 39 °C after a room-temperature cure and a flexural modulus of 2.1 GPa. It is a river-table resin. No builder in 5,000 thread posts used a clear casting resin. The thread's materials tester asks for at least 2.6 GPa from the epoxy, and says creep rises near the Tg. The same resin is planned for the rail seats and the grout, where creep under bolt load matters most. Make the post-cure mandatory, or use a laminating epoxy.
3. **The rail-seat recesses leave EG slivers over the insert bars.** The base recess (30 wide) is narrower than the bar (40 wide). This leaves 5 × 3 mm lips of EG along each bar edge. The portal leaves a 0.5 mm sliver. These cannot fill with 2–5 mm stone, so the edges will be voids or resin.
4. **The rail-seat method is the right one, but long cast-in bars bow.** DaBit cast 770 mm steel strips against a granite plate. Cure shrinkage bowed them 0.12–0.3 mm. DaBit's own conclusion was the Type 0 plan: cast in anchors, then set the precision parts later on a thin pour. Jack screws and a grout gap gave ±0.01 mm on the same machine. So keep the levelling pour, make the recess deeper, and do not expect the cast-in bars to be flat.
5. **Vibration needs a tuned frequency.** Builders found that a well-graded, low-epoxy mix barely moves on weak vibration. The German EG makers tune their vibrators between 68 and 72 Hz, set to a few tenths of a Hz. Put a speed control on the table motor and find the frequency on the test bricks.
6. **Several parts are sound or better than usual practice:** the 11% epoxy target, the heat estimate and temperature logging, the 45° haunches, the three-piece wedge plug, the grout + dowel joint, and the M5 rail threads in 10 mm steel.

## 3. Abnormalities (things few or no builders do)

| Item | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A clear river-table casting resin (Tg 39 °C) as the EG binder | **Risky.** It has good points (2 h pot life, low viscosity, self-degassing, low heat), but no EG build was found in about 5,000 thread posts. Its modulus is below the 2.6 GPa the thread's tester asks for, and it is soft at shed temperatures. |
| 10 mm webs around the portal cores | **Risky.** Below every wall rule found. |
| Rail-seat recess narrower than the insert bar (EG lips over the steel) | **Risky.** Easy to fix in CAD. |

## 4. Open questions and the tests that settle them

1. **Can the portal fill?** Cast a 300 mm long beam section (150 × 130) with the real cores, channel former and insert bar, with the planned mix and vibration. Saw it across in 2 places. Look for voids in the 10 mm webs and the lips.
2. **Vibration frequency.** On the first test brick, sweep the table speed and note the speed where the mix slumps. Measure the acceleration. Use that speed for every later pour.
3. **How much does the low Tg matter?** Cast 2 prisms, 40 × 40 × 300, one room-cured and one post-cured (and a third with a laminating epoxy if possible). Load each in 3-point bending at 30–35 °C for a week and log the deflection. Pass if the creep is small compared with the target accuracy.
4. **EG modulus with this resin.** Use the same prisms at 20 °C: measure the bending stiffness. RAMPF gives 30–35 GPa for its 5 mm grade [R1]. Members' mixes in the thread were about 14 GPa, and one well-made 10% mix was 38–40 GPa [C1 p90 #3,573][C1 p122 #4,866]. Pass if 25 GPa or more.
5. **Release.** Make three small boxes: wax on sealed MDF, wax + PVA on sealed MDF, and wax on tape or film. Cast, demould, compare.
6. **Joint thread.** Cast one 40 × 10 bar (and one 20 mm bar) in a brick with 30 mm cover. Pull the M12 rod with a torque wrench and a calibrated spring or load cell until it slips or strips.
7. **Levelling pour and bar bow.** Cast one 500 mm insert bar in a test casting. Measure the bar's bow after demoulding. Then pour a 3–5 mm deep test seat over it. Measure it after 7 days and after post-cure. Bolt a rail on for 2 weeks and measure again.
8. **Base core layout.** The core pictures are missing here. Confirm the gap between the cores and the BK/BF plates (5 mm from the numbers, 15 mm in the task text).
9. **Sources not reached.** Pages 3 and 5 of the CNCArena EG thread were not saved. Pages 42–127 were searched by topic, not read post by post. The thread refers to a German book, "Mineralguss für den Maschinenbau" (ISBN 3-478-93273-4) [C1 p125 #4,984], and to the German CNCEcke forum, where the EG makers posted their method [C1 p122 #4,877]. I did not read either. Read the MyCNCUK levelling thread (8197) when MyCNCUK is back.
10. **Number inconsistencies to tidy:** the task says the portal is 375 tall, 36 kg and the base 81 kg. The CAD says the beam top is at Z 375, portal 34.8 kg, base 78.2 kg, and `HANDOVER.md` says portal 355 H.
